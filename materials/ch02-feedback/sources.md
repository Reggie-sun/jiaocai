# Chapter 2 Source Notes

Research sources were checked on 2026-10-12. The chapter uses them to describe the named study and its stated setup only. Teaching scenarios, task decompositions, decision rules, and example values authored for this chapter are labeled as simulations; they are not study results.

| [19] | Bojarski M, et al. *End to End Learning for Self-Driving Cars*. arXiv:1604.07316 (2016). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07316) | A CNN was trained from a single forward-facing camera's raw pixels to predict steering commands; the paper describes task-learned internal representations without explicit road-feature labels. | This is the paper's system and experimental setup, not a general safety or deployment guarantee. The chapter's edge-to-texture description is explicitly an introductory analogy, not a claim that each layer has a fixed human-readable meaning. |
| [20] | Cachola I, Lo K, Cohan A, Weld D. *TLDR: Extreme Summarization of Scientific Documents*. Findings of EMNLP 2020, 4766–4777. [ACL Anthology](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.findings-emnlp.428) | Scientific-paper summarization is a studied task; the paper introduces SciTLDR and compares summarization methods on its dataset. | It does not establish that GPT outputs are factually reliable. The chapter's structured summary is a teaching template and must be checked against the source paper. |
| [21] | Arun N, et al. *Assessing the Trustworthiness of Saliency Maps for Localizing Abnormalities in Medical Imaging*. *Radiology: Artificial Intelligence*, 3(6), 2021. [RSNA](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.2021200267) | The study evaluated eight saliency methods on two public chest-radiograph datasets and found each failed at least one defined trustworthiness criterion; localization networks outperformed the saliency methods on the reported localization measures. | This supports caution about the tested saliency methods and datasets. A heatmap is not causal evidence, a diagnosis, or a general verdict on all medical AI. |
| [22] | Cui S, Lin S, Shao Z, et al. *Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning For Task Generation of Transmission Tower Inspection Using Point Clouds*. SAE Technical Paper 2026-99-0754 (published 2026-05-15). [SAE Mobilus](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/hierarchical-reinforcement-learning-task-generation-transmission-tower-inspection-using-point-clouds-2026-99-0754) | The paper proposes point-cloud processing with a high-level DQN for waypoint selection and low-level Q-learning for pose-angle optimization, with reward terms for coverage and waypoint count. | It is a proposed method and paper-specific experiment, not proof of unrestricted real-world autonomous flight. The chapter's extra high/mid/low instructional layers and safety constraints are an explicitly labeled teaching simulation, not the paper's reported architecture. |
| [23] | Dayan I, Roth H R, Zhong A, et al. *Federated learning for predicting clinical outcomes in patients with COVID-19*. *Nature Medicine*, 27(10), 1735–1743 (2021). Federated learning does not by itself guarantee privacy or equal performance at every site. |
| [24] | Zhang G, Wei C, Jing C, et al. *Short-Term Electrical Load Forecasting Based on Time Augmented Transformer*. *International Journal of Computational Intelligence Systems*, 15, 67 (2022). [Springer Nature](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44196-022-00128-y) | The paper proposes a time-augmented Transformer for short-term electric-load forecasting using historical load and additional time/weather-related variables, and evaluates it on the paper's real-world dataset. | Its reported comparisons do not guarantee superiority in every region. Attention weights do not establish that a feature caused a load change. |
| [25] | Nerem R R, Chen S, Dasgupta S, Wang Y. *Graph neural networks extrapolate out-of-distribution for shortest paths*. Proceedings of the 39th Conference on Learning Theory, PMLR 336, 5273–5331 (2026). [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/nerem26a.html) | Under the paper's sparsity-regularized loss and stated conditions, it proves a trained GNN can implement K Bellman–Ford steps and reports supporting experiments. | This is not a theorem that arbitrary trained GNNs return optimal routes on arbitrary graphs. The chapter presents explicit dynamic programming as a checkable baseline. |
| [26] | Zhang C, Song W, Cao Z, et al. *Learning to Dispatch for Job Shop Scheduling via Deep Reinforcement Learning*. NeurIPS 2020. [NeurIPS Proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/hash/11958dfee29b6709f48a9ba0387a2431-Abstract.html) | The paper learns priority dispatching rules with a deep RL agent and graph-neural representation, then compares them on the paper's scheduling benchmarks. | Benchmark results do not show that RL always beats heuristics, nor cover every production constraint or distribution shift. The two-job timing example in the chapter is an authored teaching simulation. |
| [27] | Silver D, Huang A, Maddison C J, et al. *Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search*. *Nature*, 529, 484–503 (2016). [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/mastering-the-game-of-go-with-deep-neural-networks-and-tree-search/) | AlphaGo combined policy and value networks with Monte Carlo tree search for Go. | This illustrates a Go system combining learned estimates with search; it does not imply that explicit state machines or legal-move rules are generally obsolete. |
| [28] | Wang Z, Xiao J, Wang L, Yao J. *A novel federated learning approach with knowledge transfer for credit scoring*. *Decision Support Systems*, 177, 114084 (2024). [ScienceDirect](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dss.2023.114084) | The paper proposes horizontal federated credit scoring with knowledge transfer and evaluates it on five credit datasets, including the challenge of heterogeneous and imbalanced data. | The chapter reports the paper's scope, not a universal gain for all banks. Keeping raw records local is not an absolute privacy guarantee; accuracy, calibration, group effects, and leakage risk still require evaluation.
