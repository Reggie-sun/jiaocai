# Chapter 5 Source Register

Checked on 2026-10-12. Citation numbers follow first use in the chapter. Links are written as bare URLs for export. Historical records, institutional interpretation, research findings, and the author’s teaching explanations are kept distinct. The chapter's central campus reservation story and all its people, services, scores, and exercises are teaching simulations, not institutional research or reports.

## Claim and evidence notes

- **[1] Analogy and relational mapping.** Gentner's structure-mapping framework supports the distinction between relational correspondence and surface resemblance. It is a framework for analyzing analogies, not a guarantee that a source-domain solution will work in a target domain. Body locations: 5.1.3, 5.4.2, 5.6.3.
- **[2] Gecko adhesion.** Autumn et al. report measurements and modeling related to adhesion of gecko setae. The chapter says this research supplied a mechanism-oriented engineering clue; it does not say an engineering product follows automatically from copying a gecko's appearance. Body location: 5.1.3.
- **[3] Kano quality model.** The original paper distinguishes quality attributes such as attractive and must-be quality. The chapter uses the distinction to frame questions, not as a substitute for user research or a universal ranking of features. Body location: 5.1.5.
- **[4] Design Thinking Bootleg.** The Stanford d.school page identifies empathize, define, ideate, prototype, and test as five modes and presents the cards as usable from different starting points. The chapter's explicit return arrows and iteration language are teaching organization, not a claim attributed to this page. Body location: 5.3.2 and Figure 5-3.
- **[5] WCAG 2.2.** The W3C standard includes success criteria relevant to keyboard operation, visible focus, and labels. The chapter uses these as checks, not as evidence of full conformance, a legal conclusion, or a completed user test. Body location: 5.3.4.
- **[6] Stukeley's Newton memoir.** The Newton Project transcript presents Stukeley's retrospective account of a conversation with Newton during an April 1726 visit. It is a memoir account, not a contemporaneous Newton notebook and not independent proof that a complete theory arose in one instant. Body location: 5.5.1–5.5.2 and Table 5-6.
- **[7] Royal Society manuscript catalogue.** The catalogue identifies William Stukeley's *Memoirs of Sir Isaac Newton's Life*, manuscript MS/142, dated 1752. This metadata helps identify the memoir's author and record type; it does not verify every remembered detail. Body location: 5.5.1 and Table 5-6.
- **[8] Orville Wright's 17 December 1903 diary.** The Smithsonian educational page transcribes the entry documenting the first flights, including the approximate 12-second duration and the diarist's note that the timing was not exact because the watch was not stopped promptly. The chapter treats this as a contemporaneous diary transcription. Body location: 5.5.1 and Table 5-6.
- **[9] Smithsonian 1903 Wright Flyer object page.** The National Air and Space Museum page summarizes the brothers' longer research and engineering process, including wind-tunnel work. It is a later institutional account, not a replacement for the diary or archival documents. Body location: 5.5.1 and Table 5-6.
- **[10] Jinek et al. 2012.** The Science paper investigates Cas9 in bacterial adaptive immunity and reports programmable dual-RNA-guided DNA cleavage in vitro. It is one paper in a wider research history and does not establish the safety, effectiveness, or ethical status of all later applications. Body location: 5.5.1 and Table 5-6.
- **[11] Sutton and Barto.** The second edition provides a foundational account of reinforcement learning as interaction with an environment and optimization of cumulative reward. The chapter's discussion of reward specification and social values is an explanatory caution, not an empirical result claimed by the textbook. Body location: 5.7.2.
- **[12] Pan and Yang.** This survey organizes transfer-learning settings and terminology. The chapter notes that source-target mismatch requires target-domain testing and that negative transfer is possible; it does not infer that all transfer attempts fail. Body location: 5.7.3.
- **[13] McMahan et al. 2017.** The paper abstract describes training data remaining distributed on devices and a shared model learned by aggregating locally computed updates. The chapter attributes this bounded mechanism to the paper and does not claim to have independently reviewed every privacy discussion in the full paper. Body location: 5.7.4 and Table 5-8.
- **[14] Zhu, Liu, and Han 2019.** The paper reports gradient-leakage experiments on computer-vision and natural-language-processing tasks. The chapter describes leakage under tested model/data/gradient conditions and explicitly does not generalize it to every federated protocol or model. Body location: 5.7.4 and Table 5-8.
